# Jobber Operating Manual for a UK Cleaning Company

Version: 1.0  
Prepared for: UK-based domestic and commercial cleaning operations  
Scope: Jobber setup, daily operations, field team usage, client communications, invoicing, reporting, and compliance considerations.

> Note: This manual is an operational guide, not legal, tax, employment, insurance, or health-and-safety advice. Confirm VAT, employment, insurance, chemical safety, and data-protection obligations with your accountant, solicitor, competent H&S adviser, or official UK guidance where needed.

## 1. Executive Review

Jobber is a strong fit for a cleaning company that needs to manage enquiries, quotes, recurring visits, team schedules, client communication, invoices, payments, and job history in one place. Its best use in a cleaning business is as the operating system for the whole service journey:

1. Enquiry or request comes in.
2. Office reviews the property and service need.
3. Quote is prepared and sent.
4. Client approves the quote online.
5. Work is converted into a one-off or recurring job.
6. Cleaners complete scheduled visits using the mobile app.
7. Office invoices and collects payment.
8. Client records, notes, photos, visit history, and communications stay attached to the account.

For a UK cleaning company, Jobber works especially well for:

- Regular domestic cleaning, such as weekly or fortnightly house cleans.
- End-of-tenancy cleans.
- Deep cleans and spring cleans.
- Office and commercial contract cleaning.
- Communal area cleaning for landlords or property managers.
- Carpet, oven, window, or specialist cleaning if priced as separate services.
- Site assessments where a quote is required before booking.

Main strengths:

- Clear workflow from request to quote to job to invoice.
- Recurring jobs and scheduled visits suit cleaning routes.
- Client Hub lets clients approve quotes, check appointments, pay invoices, and request more work.
- Mobile app supports field staff with schedules, visit notes, timers, and job details.
- Timesheets help track hours and support payroll preparation.
- Quote deposits can reduce no-shows and late cancellations for larger cleans.

Main limitations and watch points:

- UK VAT setup must be reviewed carefully. Jobber can support invoicing, but the company remains responsible for issuing correct VAT invoices and keeping records.
- Cleaning companies must manage UK GDPR responsibilities around customer addresses, access codes, alarm details, keys, photographs, and staff location/time data.
- Chemical records, COSHH procedures, RAMS, and accident reporting should not rely solely on Jobber unless your Jobber plan and internal process fully support them.
- Staff must be trained to use notes carefully. Never place sensitive information in client-visible notes by mistake.

Recommended operating model:

- Use Jobber as the master record for clients, jobs, schedules, quotes, invoices, visit notes, and payment status.
- Use accounting software, such as Xero, QuickBooks, or FreeAgent, as the financial ledger if required by your accountant.
- Use separate HR/payroll systems for employment records and payslips.
- Use separate H&S documentation for COSHH, risk assessments, method statements, and training records unless your Jobber configuration formally covers those items.

## 2. Roles and Permissions

Set up users by role rather than giving everyone full access.

### Owner or Director

Responsibilities:

- Account settings and billing.
- Company profile, branding, tax settings, invoice settings, and templates.
- Pricing strategy and quote approval rules.
- Reports, revenue review, and debt monitoring.
- User permissions.
- Data protection and compliance ownership.

Recommended access:

- Full access.
- Two-factor authentication where available.
- Responsible for reviewing integrations and payment settings.

### Office Manager or Administrator

Responsibilities:

- New enquiries.
- Client records.
- Quote preparation.
- Scheduling.
- Dispatch.
- Invoice creation and chasing.
- Daily exception handling.

Recommended access:

- Clients, requests, quotes, jobs, schedule, invoices, payments, and reports.
- Limited access to account billing unless needed.

### Supervisor or Team Leader

Responsibilities:

- Review daily route.
- Support cleaners.
- Check job notes, access instructions, photos, and completion quality.
- Resolve on-site issues.
- Confirm visits are complete.

Recommended access:

- Schedule, assigned jobs, team visits, client/job notes, forms, photos, and timesheets if supervising payroll inputs.
- No access to account billing or sensitive company settings.

### Cleaner or Field Operative

Responsibilities:

- Use the mobile app to check schedule.
- Arrive on time within the arrival window.
- Read job notes before starting.
- Start and stop timers if required.
- Add completion notes, issues, and photos.
- Mark visit complete.

Recommended access:

- Assigned schedule and assigned job details only.
- Internal notes relevant to the clean.
- No access to pricing reports, all-client database, or payment records unless necessary.

### Bookkeeper or Accountant

Responsibilities:

- Review invoices, payments, VAT treatment, and financial exports.
- Reconcile with accounting software.

Recommended access:

- Invoices, payments, reports, and integrations only.
- No need for operational schedule control unless agreed.

## 3. Initial Setup

### 3.1 Company Profile

Configure:

- Company legal name.
- Trading name.
- UK business address.
- Main phone number.
- Email address.
- Website.
- Logo and brand colours.
- Business hours.
- Service area, such as London, Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, or county-based coverage.

Use a professional sender identity for emails and texts. Clients should immediately recognise the business.

### 3.2 Currency and Tax

Use GBP currency.

If VAT-registered:

- Add VAT registration number to invoice templates.
- Set default VAT treatment according to your accountant's guidance.
- Ensure VAT appears correctly on VAT invoices.
- Keep copies of issued VAT invoices and VAT records.

If not VAT-registered:

- Do not issue VAT invoices.
- Do not show VAT as a separate charge.
- Consider invoice wording such as "Not VAT registered" if your accountant recommends it.

For UK records, HMRC guidance says VAT-registered businesses must keep records of VAT charged on sales and VAT paid on purchases, and must keep copies of VAT invoices issued. Only VAT-registered businesses can issue VAT invoices.

### 3.3 Services and Products

Create standard line items before quoting. This keeps pricing consistent and makes reports more useful.

Suggested service catalogue:

- Regular domestic clean - hourly.
- Regular domestic clean - fixed price.
- Deep clean - fixed price.
- End-of-tenancy clean - studio.
- End-of-tenancy clean - 1 bedroom.
- End-of-tenancy clean - 2 bedroom.
- End-of-tenancy clean - 3 bedroom.
- End-of-tenancy clean - additional bedroom.
- End-of-tenancy clean - additional bathroom.
- Office clean - per visit.
- Communal area clean - per visit.
- Carpet cleaning - per room.
- Oven cleaning - single oven.
- Fridge clean.
- Window internal clean.
- Inside cupboards.
- Limescale treatment.
- Mould treatment, if offered.
- Parking or congestion charge.
- Key collection or return.
- Out-of-area travel supplement.
- Short-notice booking surcharge.
- Cancellation fee.

Recommended line item rules:

- Use clear client-friendly names.
- Keep internal codes short, for example `DOM-REG-HR`, `EOT-2BED`, `CARPET-RM`.
- Avoid vague descriptions like "Cleaning service".
- For commercial cleaning, include frequency, site area, visit duration, and specification.

### 3.4 Quote Templates

Create templates for common cleaning jobs:

- Domestic regular clean.
- Domestic deep clean.
- End-of-tenancy clean.
- Office cleaning contract.
- Communal area cleaning.
- Carpet or specialist cleaning.

Each template should include:

- Scope of work.
- Rooms or areas included.
- Exclusions.
- Client responsibilities.
- Access requirements.
- Parking requirements.
- Payment terms.
- Cancellation policy.
- VAT position, if relevant.
- Deposit requirement, if used.

Example quote wording:

"This quote covers a one-off end-of-tenancy clean for the areas listed above. The property must be empty of personal belongings, with working electricity, running water, and safe access. Heavy waste removal, pest treatment, exterior window cleaning, wall washing, mould remediation, and carpet cleaning are excluded unless listed as separate line items."

### 3.5 Job and Visit Naming

Use visit titles that make the schedule readable. A good cleaning visit title includes client, service type, and area.

Recommended format:

`Client Name - Service Type - Postcode/Area`

Examples:

- `Smith - Regular Domestic - SW12`
- `ABC Offices - Evening Clean - M1`
- `Flat 4 - End of Tenancy - BS3`

If Jobber allows configurable visit title templates in your account, set them to show client, job title, and city or postcode area.

### 3.6 Client Hub

Client Hub is the client-facing online area. Clients can use it to approve quotes, view appointments, pay invoices, print receipts, and request more work.

Recommended configuration:

- Enable online quote approvals.
- Enable invoice payment links if using online payments.
- Use clear email and text templates.
- Add a Client Hub button to your website if useful.
- Train staff that anyone with a Client Hub link may be able to view the linked client information, so links should not be forwarded casually.

### 3.7 Communication Templates

Prepare templates for:

- Quote sent.
- Quote follow-up.
- Booking confirmation.
- Appointment reminder.
- Cleaner en route, if used.
- Job completed.
- Invoice sent.
- Invoice reminder.
- Payment receipt.
- Review request.
- Cancellation or reschedule notice.

Use British English spelling and UK-friendly tone.

Example booking confirmation:

"Your clean is booked for {{date}}. Arrival window: {{arrival_window}}. Please make sure we have safe access, running water, electricity, and any agreed parking details. If access details change, reply to this message as soon as possible."

## 4. Client Records

Client records should be consistent and complete. A poor client record creates scheduling errors, access problems, and payment delays.

### 4.1 Required Fields

For every client:

- Full name or company name.
- Billing email.
- Mobile number.
- Service address.
- Billing address, if different.
- Preferred contact method.
- Property type.
- Parking/access notes.
- Service frequency.
- Payment terms.
- VAT status for commercial clients if relevant.

### 4.2 Cleaning-Specific Notes

Record:

- Number of bedrooms and bathrooms.
- Approximate property size.
- Pets in property.
- Flooring types.
- Sensitive surfaces, such as marble, granite, brass, hardwood, or specialist finishes.
- Alarm code handling process, if required.
- Key holding status.
- Concierge or building manager details.
- Lift access.
- Parking restrictions.
- Waste disposal location.
- Products supplied by client or company.
- Areas not to clean.
- Known hazards.

### 4.3 Sensitive Information

Treat the following as sensitive:

- Alarm codes.
- Key safe codes.
- Door codes.
- Lockbox locations.
- Client absence dates.
- Vulnerability information.
- Photos inside private homes.
- Staff notes about complaints or disputes.

Rules:

- Store access details only where authorised staff can see them.
- Do not place sensitive notes in client-visible fields.
- Remove access details when no longer needed.
- Review stored access details at least quarterly.
- If a client leaves, remove or archive unnecessary access instructions.

## 5. Enquiries and Requests

Use requests for new work that has not yet become a quote or job.

### 5.1 Standard Intake Process

When an enquiry arrives:

1. Create or find the client.
2. Create a request.
3. Record service type, address, preferred dates, property details, and urgency.
4. Add photos or attachments if provided.
5. Decide whether to quote remotely, schedule an assessment, or decline.
6. Set a follow-up task if waiting for information.

### 5.2 Cleaning Enquiry Questions

Domestic regular clean:

- How many bedrooms and bathrooms?
- What is the approximate property size?
- What cleaning frequency is required?
- Are products/equipment supplied by the client or company?
- Are there pets?
- Is parking available?
- Is key holding required?

Deep clean:

- Is the property occupied or empty?
- Are inside cupboards, appliances, and windows required?
- Is there heavy limescale, mould, grease, or pet hair?
- Are carpets included?

End-of-tenancy:

- Move-out date.
- Inventory check-out date.
- Property furnished or unfurnished.
- Number of rooms and bathrooms.
- Oven, fridge/freezer, carpets, windows, and blinds.
- Access arrangements.
- Letting agent requirements.

Commercial clean:

- Site address.
- Opening hours.
- Cleaning frequency.
- Number of washrooms and kitchens.
- Approximate floor area.
- Security and alarm procedure.
- Waste handling.
- Consumables responsibility.
- Public liability or RAMS requirements.

### 5.3 Request Status Rules

- New: enquiry received but not reviewed.
- Awaiting info: client needs to provide details/photos.
- Assessment scheduled: site visit booked.
- Quote required: enough information to quote.
- Converted: request converted to quote or job.
- Declined: work outside scope, area, availability, or risk appetite.

## 6. Quotes

Quotes should be specific enough to prevent disputes.

### 6.1 When to Quote

Create a quote for:

- Most end-of-tenancy cleans.
- Deep cleans.
- One-off domestic cleans.
- Commercial contracts.
- Jobs requiring deposits.
- Jobs with optional extras.

For simple recurring hourly domestic cleaning, you may create either:

- A quote first, then convert to recurring job after approval.
- A job directly if the price and scope are already agreed.

### 6.2 Quote Structure

Every quote should include:

- Client and property address.
- Service date or proposed frequency.
- Scope of work.
- Line items.
- Optional extras.
- Deposit, if required.
- Payment terms.
- Cancellation policy.
- Exclusions.
- Validity period.

### 6.3 Optional Extras

Use optional line items where possible so clients can choose extras without a long email chain.

Common optional extras:

- Oven clean.
- Fridge/freezer clean.
- Carpet cleaning.
- Internal windows.
- Inside cupboards.
- Blinds.
- Wall spot cleaning.
- Balcony/patio sweep.
- Additional bathroom.
- Additional bedroom.

### 6.4 Deposits

Use deposits for:

- End-of-tenancy cleans.
- Large deep cleans.
- Commercial first cleans.
- Short-notice bookings.
- High-risk clients or previous late payments.

Suggested deposit rules:

- 25% to 50% for large one-off cleans.
- Full payment upfront for small one-off domestic jobs if your policy allows.
- No deposit for established recurring clients unless there is a risk reason.

In Jobber, deposits can be attached to quotes and applied later to invoices. If online payments are enabled, clients may be able to pay the deposit through Client Hub.

### 6.5 Quote Approval Workflow

1. Create quote from request or client profile.
2. Add line items and optional extras.
3. Add internal notes for staff, if needed.
4. Preview as client.
5. Send by email or text.
6. Follow up after the configured number of days.
7. When approved, convert to job.
8. Archive or close stale quotes after the agreed validity period.

### 6.6 Quote Review Checklist

Before sending:

- Client name and address are correct.
- Scope is specific.
- Exclusions are stated.
- VAT is correct, if applicable.
- Deposit is correct.
- Dates and arrival windows are realistic.
- Optional extras are priced correctly.
- Internal notes are not visible to the client.

## 7. Jobs and Scheduling

Jobs are the operational record of approved work.

### 7.1 One-Off Jobs

Use one-off jobs for:

- End-of-tenancy cleans.
- Deep cleans.
- Spring cleans.
- Carpet cleans.
- Oven cleans.
- Ad hoc commercial cleans.

Process:

1. Convert approved quote to job or create job directly.
2. Add job title.
3. Confirm service address.
4. Add visit date and arrival window.
5. Assign cleaner or team.
6. Add internal instructions.
7. Attach photos or checklists.
8. Save and send booking confirmation.

### 7.2 Recurring Jobs

Use recurring jobs for:

- Weekly domestic cleaning.
- Fortnightly domestic cleaning.
- Daily or weekly office cleaning.
- Communal area contracts.
- Monthly specialist cleans.

Recurring job setup:

- Frequency.
- Start date.
- Visit duration.
- Assigned team.
- Billing schedule.
- Fixed monthly price or per-visit price.
- Public holidays policy.
- Cancellation and notice period.

Typical options:

- Domestic weekly clean: invoice after each visit or monthly in arrears.
- Office contract: fixed monthly invoice.
- Communal area clean: fixed monthly invoice.
- Fortnightly domestic clean: invoice per visit.

### 7.3 Arrival Windows

Use arrival windows to avoid over-promising exact times.

Recommended:

- Domestic recurring clean: 1-hour or 2-hour arrival window.
- End-of-tenancy clean: morning arrival window.
- Commercial clean: fixed slot if access/security requires it.

Example:

"Arrival window: 8:00-9:00. Clean expected duration: approximately 5 hours depending on property condition."

### 7.4 Route Planning

For each day:

- Group jobs by postcode or area.
- Put key-holding jobs where timing is flexible.
- Put access-critical jobs first.
- Allow travel and parking time.
- Avoid scheduling deep cleans after a full domestic route.
- Assign two-person teams for large jobs.

Daily office review:

1. Check tomorrow's schedule by 15:00.
2. Confirm access details.
3. Confirm staff availability.
4. Check deposits or prepayments.
5. Send reminders.
6. Resolve missing information before close of business.

## 8. Mobile App Procedure for Cleaners

### 8.1 Start of Day

Cleaner should:

1. Open Jobber app.
2. Check assigned schedule.
3. Confirm first address and arrival window.
4. Review internal notes.
5. Check equipment and products.
6. Clock in or start general timer if company policy requires.

### 8.2 Before Entering Property

Cleaner should check:

- Correct address.
- Access notes.
- Alarm or key procedure.
- Pets.
- Hazards.
- Client-specific preferences.
- Areas excluded from cleaning.

If access fails:

1. Call client.
2. Message office.
3. Wait according to company policy.
4. Add note to job.
5. Take evidence if appropriate, such as timestamped arrival note.

### 8.3 During Visit

Cleaner should:

- Start visit timer if required.
- Follow checklist or job form.
- Add notes for anything unusual.
- Take photos only if authorised and necessary.
- Report damage, safety concerns, pest issues, mould, bodily fluids, or unsafe access immediately.

### 8.4 Completion

Before leaving:

1. Complete checklist.
2. Add completion note.
3. Upload required photos.
4. Stop timer.
5. Mark visit complete.
6. Lock property and return key according to procedure.
7. Report any issue that may affect invoicing or client satisfaction.

Example completion note:

"Clean completed. Kitchen and bathrooms deep cleaned. Heavy limescale remains on lower shower screen despite treatment; recommend specialist descaling on next visit. Key returned to lockbox."

### 8.5 Do Not Put in Notes

Avoid:

- Personal opinions about clients.
- Sensitive medical or family information unless operationally necessary and approved.
- Unprofessional language.
- Passwords or codes in fields visible to clients.
- Photos of people, documents, valuables, or children.

## 10. Invoicing and Payments

### 10.1 Invoice Timing

Recommended invoice timing:

- One-off domestic clean: invoice immediately after completion or collect upfront.
- End-of-tenancy clean: invoice before or immediately after completion, depending on policy.
- Regular domestic cleaning: invoice per visit, weekly, fortnightly, or monthly.
- Commercial cleaning: invoice monthly according to contract.

### 10.2 Payment Terms

Typical terms:

- Domestic one-off: due on receipt.
- Regular domestic: due on receipt or within 7 days.
- Commercial: 7, 14, or 30 days depending on contract.
- Late-payment process should be documented.

### 10.3 Invoice Checklist

Before sending:

- Client name and billing address are correct.
- Service address is clear.
- Service date or billing period is shown.
- Line items match completed work.
- Deposit has been applied.
- VAT treatment is correct.
- Payment link works if online payments are enabled.
- Internal notes are not visible.

### 10.4 VAT Invoice Considerations

If VAT-registered, confirm invoice template includes:

- Business name and address.
- VAT registration number.
- Invoice number.
- Invoice date.
- Tax point, if different.
- Customer name and address.
- Description of services.
- Net amount.
- VAT rate.
- VAT amount.
- Total amount.

Keep copies of VAT invoices and maintain VAT records according to HMRC guidance.

### 10.5 Late Payment Workflow

1. Invoice sent.
2. Reminder after due date.
3. Personal follow-up after 7 days overdue.
4. Suspend future one-off bookings if unpaid.
5. For commercial clients, follow contract terms.
6. Escalate to formal debt process if required.

Suggested reminder wording:

"Just a quick reminder that invoice {{invoice_number}} for {{amount}} is now due. You can pay securely using the link in the invoice. Please let us know if you have any questions."

## 11. Timesheets and Payroll Preparation

Use Jobber timesheets to understand hours worked, job labour, travel time, and payroll preparation.

### 11.1 Time Tracking Policy

Decide whether staff should track:

- Visit time only.
- Full working day.
- Travel time.
- Breaks.
- Assessment time.

Document the policy clearly. Time entries may affect pay calculations, labour costing, and client profitability.

### 11.2 Cleaner Procedure

Cleaner should:

- Clock in at start of day if required.
- Start visit timer at the property.
- Stop visit timer when work is complete.
- Add notes for delays, access issues, or extra work.
- Clock out at end of day if using general timers.

### 11.3 Office Review

Daily:

- Check missing timers.
- Check unusually long or short visits.
- Check access failures.
- Check jobs marked complete without notes.

Weekly:

- Review timesheets.
- Correct approved manual entries.
- Confirm payroll inputs.
- Compare labour hours to job pricing.

Jobber documentation notes that timers in the mobile app can feed timesheets, and GPS waypoints may be recorded for timer-based entries. If using location-based visibility, tell staff what is collected and why.

## 12. Quality Control

### 12.1 Quality Notes

Use internal notes to record:

- Client preferences.
- Previous complaints.
- Areas requiring extra attention.
- Product restrictions.
- Supervisor checks.

Keep notes factual and professional.

### 12.2 Complaint Workflow

1. Log complaint on client/job record.
2. Add photos if provided.
3. Review visit notes and cleaner comments.
4. Decide whether revisit, refund, goodwill credit, or no action is appropriate.
5. Schedule corrective visit if needed.
6. Record resolution.

Example internal complaint note:

"Client reported dust remaining on bedroom skirting boards after 12 May visit. Supervisor reviewed photos. Revisit booked for 14 May, no charge. Add bedroom skirting boards to priority notes for next 4 visits."

### 12.3 Supervisor Audits

For recurring clients:

- Audit new cleaners within first two weeks.
- Audit commercial sites monthly.
- Audit high-value domestic clients quarterly.
- Record inspection results.

## 13. Data Protection and Security

A cleaning company handles personal data such as names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, property access notes, photographs, and payment/invoice records. Some records may be especially sensitive because they reveal when a person is away from home or how to access a property.

### 13.1 UK GDPR Principles in Practice

Apply these rules:

- Collect only what you need.
- Use data only for running the cleaning service.
- Keep records accurate.
- Remove information when no longer needed.
- Restrict staff access based on role.
- Protect devices and accounts.
- Be ready to explain how data is used.

### 13.2 Staff Security Rules

- Use named accounts, not shared logins.
- Use strong passwords.
- Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
- Remove access immediately when staff leave.
- Do not export client lists unless required.
- Do not store client data in personal notebooks or personal phones.
- Do not share screenshots of Jobber records in informal messaging groups.

### 13.3 Mobile Device Rules

- Phones must have screen locks.
- Lost devices must be reported immediately.
- Staff should not save client photos outside approved systems.
- Staff should not share client addresses with unauthorised people.

### 13.4 Data Retention

Suggested retention approach:

- Active clients: keep operational records.
- Former clients: retain financial records as required; remove access codes and unnecessary operational notes.
- Quote-only prospects: archive or delete after a defined period if no longer needed.
- Staff location/time records: retain only as long as needed for payroll, dispute, and business purposes.

Confirm exact retention periods with your accountant and data protection adviser.

## 14. Health, Safety, and Cleaning Risk Controls

Jobber can support operational records, but it should not replace a proper health-and-safety system.

### 14.1 Cleaning Hazards to Record

- Slips, trips, and wet floors.
- Working at height.
- Electrical hazards.
- Biohazards.
- Mould.
- Pest activity.
- Aggressive pets.
- Unsafe parking or access.
- Lone working risks.
- Chemical use.
- Manual handling.

### 14.2 COSHH and Chemical Use

Maintain separate COSHH assessments and safety data sheets for cleaning products unless your Jobber account is specifically configured and validated for chemical tracking.

Operational Jobber notes can record:

- Product restrictions at client site.
- "Do not use bleach."
- "Use client-supplied eco products only."
- "Marble worktop: neutral pH cleaner only."

Do not rely on short job notes as the only COSHH training record.

### 14.3 Incident Process

If damage, injury, theft allegation, or safety incident occurs:

1. Stop work if unsafe.
2. Notify office immediately.
3. Record factual note in Jobber.
4. Take photos if appropriate and lawful.
5. Complete company incident form.
6. Notify insurer if required.
7. Follow RIDDOR or other reporting obligations if applicable.

5. Add access and pet notes.

### SOP 2: End-of-Tenancy Clean

2. Gather property details and photos.
3. Create quote with scope and exclusions.
4. Add optional extras.
5. Add deposit.
6. Send quote.
7. Convert approved quote to one-off job.
8. Schedule team and duration.
9. Confirm access, parking, utilities, and empty property.
10. Complete checklist and photos.
11. Invoice balance.
12. Follow up for review.

### SOP 3: Commercial Cleaning Contract

1. Create client and site record.
2. Schedule assessment.
3. Record site specification.
4. Create quote with frequency, tasks, consumables, and terms.
5. Convert approved quote to recurring job.
6. Set monthly billing schedule.
7. Assign team and supervisor.
8. Add security/access process.
9. Create site checklist.
10. Review first month performance.
11. Schedule regular account reviews.

### SOP 4: Access Failure

1. Cleaner records arrival time.
2. Cleaner calls client.
3. Cleaner contacts office.
4. Office attempts secondary contact.
5. Cleaner waits according to company policy.
6. Add note to visit.
7. Mark as unsuccessful or reschedule according to policy.
8. Apply cancellation/access fee if contract allows.

### SOP 5: Extra Work Requested On Site

1. Cleaner records request.
2. Cleaner does not promise work if time/price is unclear.
3. Office reviews request.
4. Add line item to job or create quote.
5. Client approves price.
6. Schedule or complete extra work.
7. Invoice accordingly.

## 19. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Creating jobs without enough property detail.
- Putting access codes in client-visible notes.
- Sending quotes without exclusions.
- Forgetting to apply deposits to final invoices.
- Allowing cleaners to mark visits complete without notes.
- Using exact arrival times when a window is more realistic.
- Not checking VAT invoice formatting.
- Not removing ex-staff access.
- Relying on memory instead of Jobber notes.
- Letting old quotes stay open indefinitely.
